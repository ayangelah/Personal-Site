    • [Github] [Writeup]
      Adversarial Training AI Safety nanoGCG LoRA

      I compared static and dynamic adversarial training on GPT-2 using LoRA and nanoGCG, inspired by HarmBench's R2D2 approach. With eight training behaviors, four held-out behaviors, and a matched GCG training budget, I tested whether refreshing attacks against the evolving model improved resistance to fresh attacks. Both defenses reached 0% attack success, but dynamic training made the targets harder to elicit: mean target negative log-likelihood rose to 21.25, compared with 14.45 for static training. This single-seed toy study also exposed a limitation of exact-match evaluation: semantically equivalent outputs can count as failed attacks.

    • [Github]
      LoRA Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning Controlled Ablations PyTorch & PEFT

      I explored Low-Rank Adaptation by fine-tuning GPT-2 on Tiny Shakespeare while keeping the pretrained weights frozen. I inspected PEFT's trainable parameters, verified gradient flow, and compared adapter ranks 2, 8, and 32 using the same data split, seed, batch order, and training budget. All three configurations improved held-out language-modeling loss while training less than 1% of the model's parameters. Rank 32 reduced validation loss from 4.4445 to 3.5920, with diminishing gains as adapter capacity increased. This small, single-seed study helped me understand parameter-efficient adaptation and how to design controlled ablations.

    • [Github]
      Greedy Coordinate Gradient Discrete Optimization GPT-2 PyTorch

      I built a small GPT-2 experiment to explore gradient-guided search over discrete tokens. Starting with an editable suffix and a benign one-token target, I projected input-embedding gradients onto vocabulary embeddings, proposed token substitutions, and evaluated each candidate's actual target loss. I compared this approach with random token search under the same candidate-evaluation budget. Across three seeds, gradient-guided search reached an average final loss of 3.167, compared with 6.600 for random search. The experiment illustrates GCG's core intuition, while remaining limited to one prompt, one model, and a single target token.

    • 6/20/26 to 6/27/26 [Github]
      Mechanistic Interpretability Transformer Circuits Activation Patching TransformerLens

      A systematic analysis of all 144 attention heads in GPT-2 Small (12 layers × 12 heads): what each head does, and whether the model causally depends on it. I combine correlational QK/OV circuit scoring (induction, previous-token, copy, and copy-suppression scores) with causal activation patching (forward hooks on hook_z, splicing clean activations into corrupted runs). The analysis recovers the canonical circuit: induction heads at L5H1 and L5H5, a previous-token head at L4H11, and a copy-suppression head at L10H7. A key finding: induction is carried redundantly across a five-head cluster; patching any single head recovers only ~10% of the behavior, evidence of distributed rather than localized circuits. Built with TransformerLens.

    • 2/1/25 to 2/2/25 [Github]
      Generative Audio Audio Embeddings Vector Similarity Search Multimodal

      Serene Stream encourages you to engage with their surroundings by recording sound bytes: bird calls, water features, leaf rustling, etc. After saving a few clips, these clips can be remixed with a chosen prompt into a personalized AI-generated lofi song. Each finished track is embedded as a vector and compared against anonymous tracks from all over the world, surfacing the ones most similar to yours, fostering a sense of connection between nature lovers everywhere.

      Inspiration: nature has always been a balm for mental health, especially important for the LGBTQ community, which experiences disproportionately higher rates of depression and anxiety. We wanted to combine attachment to local greenspaces with investment in one's own mental health.

      This project was an entry and first place winner in the 2025 QWER Hacks hackathon's Sustainability category, as well as winner for Most Creative and Best Use of Gen AI superlative prizes.

    • 3/5/23 [Github]
      Supervised Learning Model Training Cloud Model Serving Real-Time Inference

      We wanted to see if we could use machine learning to teach the general populace, especially in a city as traffic-prone as LA, good driving habits and attitudes to prevent road rage incidents. RoadRager is a 2D-style game in which the user controls a vehicle behind another car, and must avoid driving too aggressively as weather and road conditions constantly change. A slider rates how aggressively they're driving: the driving data is streamed to a Google Cloud endpoint, where it's scored against a machine learning model of our own design, trained on real-world driving data.

    • 1/28/23 to 1/29/23 [Github]

      Dr. AMA is a web-based medical chat app created to fight discrimination against the LGBTQ+ community. Dr. AMA allows users to create their own profile that fits with their social identity and medical needs while providing anonymity and safeguarding against bias.
